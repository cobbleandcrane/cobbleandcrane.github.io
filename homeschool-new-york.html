How to homeschool in New York (2026-27)

New York runs on a yearly cycle: a notice of intent, an instruction plan (the IHIP), four quarterly reports and a year-end assessment. The paperwork is regular but predictable.

Key dates

  • July 1Send your notice of intent to the superintendent (or within 14 days if you start mid-year).
  • Within 10 business daysThe district sends you the regulation and an IHIP form.
  • 4 weeks later, or August 15 (whichever is later)Send back your completed IHIP.
  • Four dates you choose in the IHIPSend a quarterly report for each child.
  • With the 4th quarterly reportSend the annual assessment (test or written narrative).

Step 1: Notice of intent

Send a written notice to the superintendent of your school district of residence by July 1 each school year. If you start home instruction (or move into the district) after the year begins, send it within 14 days of starting.

Step 2: The IHIP (Individualized Home Instruction Plan)

Due within four weeks of receiving the district's form, or by August 15, whichever is later. It lists:

  • Your child's name, age and grade
  • The syllabi, curriculum materials, textbooks or plan of instruction for each required subject
  • The dates of your four quarterly reports (spaced evenly through the year)
  • The names of the people providing instruction

What to teach

  • Grades 1 to 6: arithmetic, reading, spelling, writing, English, geography, U.S. history, science, health, music, visual arts, physical education (plus bilingual education or English as a second language where needed)
  • Grades 7 and 8 (over both years): English, history and geography, science and mathematics (2 units each), art and music (1/2 unit each), plus physical education, health, practical arts and library skills on a regular basis
  • Grades 9 to 12 (over four years): English (4 units), social studies (4), mathematics (2), science (2), art and/or music (1), health (1/2), physical education (2), electives (3)

Some topics must be covered at least once in grades 1 to 8, including U.S. and New York history and both constitutions.

How much

The substantial equivalent of 180 days: 900 hours a year in grades 1 to 6, 990 hours in grades 7 to 12. Keep attendance records and show them if the district asks.

Quarterly reports

  • Hours of instruction that quarter
  • What was covered in each subject
  • A grade or a short written narrative for each subject
  • A written explanation if less than 80% of the planned material for the quarter was covered in any subject

End of year: the assessment

Send it with the 4th quarterly report. Either a standardized test (an approved test with a composite above the 33rd percentile, or one year of growth), or a written narrative from a New York-certified teacher, a peer review panel or another approved person. Narratives are allowed any year in grades 1 to 3, every other year in grades 4 to 8, and not in grades 9 to 12. Falling short puts the program on probation for up to two years with a remediation plan.

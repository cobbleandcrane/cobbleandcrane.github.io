Christmas activities for 3-year-olds: 15 easy ideas for home

By Cobble & Crane · November 22, 2026

Three-year-olds love Christmas for the small stuff: tape, stickers, lights and the box the present came in. A basket of supplies and a handful of ten-minute ideas will carry you through December. Below are fifteen activities that suit this age, a simple daily plan for the weeks before Christmas, and pages you can print tonight.

What to expect at age 3

Plan on ten to fifteen minutes per activity. Most three-year-olds will ask for the same one again the next day, so a short list goes a long way.

  • Big movements first: chunky crayons, wide tracing paths and thick cutting strips. Small details get easier around four and five.
  • Let the craft look like a three-year-old made it: the ornament will be a blob, and they'll be proud of it anyway.
  • Offer a choice: "Tracing or cutting?" gets a better answer than "Time for a worksheet."
  • Keep small parts at the table with you: bells, pom-poms and beads are fine when you're sitting there, not in a bin they can reach alone.

Set up a Christmas basket

Fill one basket or shoebox and keep it on a high shelf. Bring it down at activity time and put it back after, so it stays special.

  • Chunky crayons or washable markers in red and green
  • A glue stick and child-safe scissors
  • Star stickers, dot stickers and a roll of painter's tape
  • Scraps of wrapping paper, ribbon and old Christmas cards
  • A folder of printed pages
Painter's tape earns its spotGive a three-year-old a roll of it and an empty box and you'll get twenty minutes of "wrapping." It peels off tables and floors cleanly when they're done.

Fifteen Christmas activities for 3-year-olds

The ones with an orange edge are printables. The rest use things you already have at home.

1. A countdown card every morning Printable

One small idea a day gives December a shape: read a story by the tree, make a paper chain link, walk a drawing to a neighbor. Our Christmas Countdown Cards have 24 of these ideas, plus a countdown road to color in one square at a time.

2. Trace candy canes and snowy hills Printable

Tracing paths shaped like candy cane hooks, rolling hills and twinkle-light zigzags practice the strokes kids use for letters later. Wide paths matter at three: a wobbly crayon still stays on the road.

3. Count the ornaments No prep

Count the ornaments on the bottom branches, then only the red ones, then only the round ones. Stop at five or ten and start over.

4. Decorate a paper tree 5 min setup

Draw a big triangle tree on paper, or tape one to the wall at their height. Hand over dot stickers for ornaments and a star for the top. Peeling stickers off the sheet is fine-motor practice in disguise.

5. Trim Santa's beard Printable

Snipping strips into a beard makes a good first scissor job: one cut per strip and no turning the paper. Sit with them and use child-safe scissors. Our Trim Santa's Beard pages start with straight snips and work up to wavy and zigzag beards.

6. Wrap a box No prep

Give them an empty box, scraps of wrapping paper and tape. The result will look more taped than wrapped, and they'll want to give it to someone, so pick a lucky grandparent.

7. Playdough cookies 5 min setup

Roll out playdough and cut shapes with real cookie cutters. Add a muffin tin and a spatula and you have a bakery. Big, simple cutters work best for small hands.

8. A Christmas lights walk No prep

After dinner, bundle up and look at the lights on your street, or drive a loop in pajamas. Count the houses with lights, find a blue one, spot a reindeer. It burns off the before-bed wiggles too.

9. Sort ornaments by color No prep

Pull out the unbreakable ornaments and set out three bowls. Sort by color, then by size. Hang them back on the tree afterward and it counts as decorating.

10. Drive the tree home Printable

A maze with a tree truck at the start and a house at the end. Let them try it with a finger first, then a crayon. Four or five turns is plenty at this age.

11. Jingle bell freeze dance No prep

Play Christmas music and dance. When the music stops, everyone freezes like a snowman. Bells sealed inside a taped-shut plastic bottle make a shaker with no loose parts.

12. Make a card for someone Printable

Color a card, add a sticker and let them sign it with a scribble. Walking it to a neighbor's door or dropping it in the mailbox finishes the job.

13. Deliver presents by sleigh No prep

A laundry basket becomes a sleigh. Wrap a few blocks in paper and let them deliver a present to every stuffed animal in the house. Keep going until the reindeer (you) need a break.

14. Paper chain countdown 5 min setup

You cut strips of red and green paper; they tape or glue them into loops. Tear off one loop each night. Ask for red, green, red, green and you've snuck in a pattern lesson.

15. Color while you wrap Printable

When you need twenty quiet minutes to wrap their presents, set them up at the table with a snowplow to color or a tree to decorate. Pages with big, simple shapes suit three-year-olds better than detailed coloring books.

A simple plan for December

Three small moments a day is plenty:

  • Morning: read the countdown card together.
  • Afternoon: one table activity from the basket, ten to fifteen minutes.
  • Evening: something with movement, like freeze dance or a lights walk.

Rotate printables, crafts and no-prep games so the basket feels new each time. On a busy day, the countdown card alone is enough.

Christmas Day and the kids' table

A holiday dinner runs long for a three-year-old. Put a coloring placemat and a few crayons at each kid's seat and they'll have something to do between courses. Our Christmas Placemats each have coloring, a maze and tic-tac-toe, so older cousins can play too. For the grown-ups and big kids, a giant word search poster on the wall gives the whole family something to hunt for together.

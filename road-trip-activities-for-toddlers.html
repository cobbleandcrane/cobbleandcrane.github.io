Screen-free road trip activities for toddlers and preschoolers

By Cobble & Crane · November 21, 2026

A long drive with a two- to five-year-old goes better with a small plan: one bag within reach, a handful of activities that work on a bumpy lap, and a rule that new things come out slowly. Here is what we pack, the twelve activities that hold up best in a car seat, and what to expect at each age.

Pack a busy bag first

Keep it in the front seat so you control the pace, not in the trunk and not in the child's lap.

  • A flat surface: a clipboard or a small cookie sheet works as a lap desk, and magnets stick to the cookie sheet.
  • Something to draw with: twist-up crayons or washable markers in a pencil pouch. Regular crayons soften in a hot car, so keep them out of the sun.
  • Stickers and painter's tape: both peel off car windows and seat backs without a fight.
  • A folder of printed pages: about one page for every half hour of driving.
  • Wipes and a small trash bag for the snack that always follows.
Hand out one thing at a timeA new page every half hour feels like a surprise. The whole folder at once is usually over in ten minutes.

Twelve screen-free car activities

The ones marked with an orange edge are printables; the rest need nothing but you.

1. I spy colors Age 2+

"I spy something red." For two-year-olds, name the color and let them point at anything that matches. For older kids, take turns being the spy.

2. Road trip bingo Age 3+

A grid of pictures: car, stop sign, bus, truck, tree, gas pump. Color a square when you spot one; the first row wins the next snack. There's a free bingo page in our free sampler.

3. Count one thing Age 3+

Pick one thing to count for the next ten minutes: red cars, cows, bridges or trucks. Younger kids count to five and start over; older kids keep a tally on paper.

4. Tracing roads on a clipboard Age 3+

Wide tracing paths still work when the car bumps, which thin lines don't. Drive a crayon from the truck to the cone, over bridges and around loops.

5. Sticker roads Age 2+

Draw a long wiggly road on paper and let them line stickers along it. It's the best fine-motor activity for a two-year-old in a car seat.

6. Mazes Age 4+

Start with mazes that have four or five turns. A finger works before a crayon does, and a finished maze is a good moment to hand over the next page.

7. Dot to dots Age 4+

Dot to dots from 1 to 10 practice counting and pencil control at the same time, and the picture at the end is the reward.

8. Draw what you see Age 3+

Fold a sheet into four boxes. At each stop or every twenty minutes, they draw one thing from the window. By the end it's a little comic of the trip.

9. Audiobook plus coloring Age 2+

A story on the speakers and a coloring page in the lap is the closest thing to quiet time a car gets. Pick stories they already know so they can follow along.

10. Where are we going? Age 4+

A simple map page: draw home on one side and the destination on the other, then add a sticker or a mark at every stop. It answers "are we there yet" before it's asked.

11. Silly song swap Age 2+

Sing a song they know and swap the words for things outside: "The wheels on the truck," "The cows in the field." Kids love correcting you.

12. Snack stops Age 2+

Pack one small snack bag per hour, labeled with a number. "Snack two comes after the bridge" gives little kids a way to measure time.

What works at each age

Age 2

Short and hands-on: stickers, crayons, songs and I spy colors. Expect five to ten minutes per activity, and plan more stops than you think you need.

Age 3

Tracing, counting and picture bingo start to work, as long as the pages are simple: one idea per page, big pictures, thick lines.

Ages 4 and 5

Mazes, dot to dots, bingo races between siblings and drawing the trip. Many four- and five-year-olds can work through several pages in a row.

Planes, restaurants and waiting rooms

The same bag works anywhere you have to wait. Fold three or four pages into a coat pocket, bring two crayons and skip anything that rolls. On a plane, painter's tape and stickers on the tray table buy a surprising amount of time.

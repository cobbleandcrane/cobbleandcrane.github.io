Indoor snow day activities for preschoolers: 15 ideas and a sample day

By Cobble & Crane · November 27, 2026

School's closed, the roads are white, and it's 8 a.m. with a whole day ahead. A snow day with a two- to five-year-old goes better with a loose plan: a few hands-on activities, one messy one, some quiet table time, and a trip outside while they still want to go. Here are fifteen indoor snow day activities that use what you already have, plus a sample day you can copy.

Bring the snow inside

1. Snow in a bin Age 2+

Fill a plastic bin or a baking sheet with real snow and set it on a towel. Hand over mittens, measuring cups, spoons and small toy trucks. It melts in about twenty minutes, which is about as long as it holds a toddler's attention anyway.

2. Snow painting Age 2+

Fill a squeeze bottle or spray bottle with water and a few drops of food coloring, and paint the snow in the bin. Do this one in the bathtub or over a towel.

3. Ice excavation Age 3+

The night before (or first thing in the morning), freeze small plastic toys in a container of water. Kids dig them out with spoons, salt and droppers of warm water. Construction-loving kids will happily call it a dig site.

4. Snowball counting Age 3+

Write numbers 1 to 10 on cups and have kids drop that many cotton balls ("snowballs") into each with tongs or a spoon. Counting and hand strength at once.

Move around (without breaking anything)

5. Indoor snowball fight Age 2+

Rolled-up socks or crumpled paper make soft snowballs. Build a couch-cushion wall on each side and go. Clean-up is part of the game: whoever gets the most snowballs back in the basket wins.

6. Snowplow obstacle course Age 3+

Kids push a laundry basket "plow" across the floor to clear paper snowballs into a pile, around cushions and under a table tunnel. Time a few runs.

7. Freeze dance Age 2+

Play music; when it stops, everyone freezes like a snowman. Add rules as they get better: freeze on one foot, freeze with a funny face.

8. Blanket fort Age 3+

Chairs, blankets and clothespins. Once it's built, it becomes the place for books, snacks and quiet time for the rest of the day.

Quiet table time

9. Snowy road tracing Age 3+

Drive a crayon along wide winter roads: from the plow to the garage, around the snowman, over the bridge. Good pencil practice that doesn't feel like school.

10. Snow day bingo Age 3+

A picture bingo card of snowy things: mittens, a plow, a sled, a snowman, a hot cocoa mug. Look out the window and mark what you can see, or call the pictures out loud.

11. Paper snowflakes Age 4+

Fold a square of paper into a triangle twice, snip shapes along the edges and unfold. Younger kids can snip straight fringe on strips of paper instead and still feel like they cut snow.

12. Playdough snowmen Age 2+

White playdough, three balls, and buttons, beads or dry pasta for the face (supervise small pieces). Count the balls, line them up biggest to smallest, then squash and start over.

13. Contact paper snow window Age 2+

Tape a sheet of contact paper to a window, sticky side out. Kids press on cotton balls, paper snowflakes and tissue squares. It stays up all week.

Warm-up activities

14. Hot cocoa counting Age 3+

Make cocoa together and count the marshmallows into each mug. Talk about hot and cold while it cools: what happens to a marshmallow, what happens to the snow in the bin.

15. Snowy story time Age 2+

Pile into the fort with a stack of winter picture books. Then ask them to tell you a story about their own snow day, and write it down for them in a few sentences.

Prep the night before a stormFreeze the ice excavation toys, set a stack of printed pages on the counter, and put a bin by the door for snow. Ten minutes the night before buys you a much easier morning.

A sample snow day

  • Morning: snow in a bin and snow painting while the snow is fresh, then breakfast.
  • Mid-morning: outside to play, or the snowplow obstacle course if it's too cold.
  • Before lunch: ice excavation at the kitchen sink.
  • After lunch: quiet time in the fort with books, or a nap for little ones.
  • Afternoon: table time with tracing, bingo and snowflakes, then playdough snowmen.
  • Late afternoon: indoor snowball fight and freeze dance to burn off the rest.
  • Evening: hot cocoa counting and a snow day story.

What works at each age

Age 2

Sensory play wins: snow in a bin, playdough, the contact paper window and anything they can dump and refill. Expect five to ten minutes per activity.

Age 3

Counting games, simple tracing, bingo with pictures and the obstacle course. Threes love being in charge of the cocoa marshmallows.

Ages 4 and 5

Paper snowflakes, ice excavation with tools, timed obstacle runs and telling their own snow day story. Many fours and fives can work through a few printed pages in a row.

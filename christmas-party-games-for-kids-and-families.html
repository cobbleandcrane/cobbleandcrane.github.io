Christmas party games for kids and families: 15 easy games for every age

By Cobble & Crane · November 23, 2026

A good Christmas party game is one the four-year-old and Grandpa can both play, needs nothing you have to buy, and is over before anyone melts down. Below are fifteen games that fit: quick ones for little kids, games the whole family plays together, and a couple of printable hunts that keep people busy while dinner finishes. There's also a 45-minute plan you can copy as-is.

Before you start: three rules that save the party

  • No knockouts under age five: games where kids sit out ("you're out!") end in tears. Change the rule so everyone keeps playing and the round just ends.
  • Team up across ages: pair a little kid with a teen or a grandparent. The little one gets help, the big one has a reason to play, and nobody wins by being older.
  • Keep rounds short: five minutes a game is plenty. Stop while people still want one more round.
Prizes are optionalA sheet of stickers or a candy cane for everyone who plays works better than one big prize. Little kids care about getting something, not about winning.

Games for little kids (ages 2 to 5)

1. Freeze dance No prep

Play Christmas music. When it stops, everyone freezes like a snowman. Anyone who wobbles does a silly reindeer pose instead of sitting out, then the music starts again.

2. Candy cane hunt 5 min setup

Hide wrapped candy canes (or paper ones) around one room, low enough for small kids to spot. Give each child a cup and a number to find, so the fastest hunter doesn't take them all.

3. Sock snowball toss No prep

Roll white socks into balls and toss them into a laundry basket or a bucket. Little kids stand close; big kids step back a giant step each round. Soft enough for indoors.

4. Pass the present 5 min setup

Wrap a small gift in several layers of paper. Pass it around the circle while music plays; when it stops, whoever holds it tears off one layer. Put a sticker or a chocolate between the layers so more kids get something.

5. Reindeer, reindeer, Santa No prep

Duck, duck, goose with a Christmas name. The "Santa" chases the tapper around the circle. It needs no supplies and three-year-olds pick it up right away.

Games the whole family can play

6. Giant word search on the wall Printable

Hang a big word search poster where people gather and leave a few markers next to it. Guests circle words as they walk past, and it keeps going all evening with no one in charge. Our Giant Christmas Word Search has 80 words in a 30 by 30 grid, prints at 24 by 36 or 18 by 24 at any print shop, and comes with a letter-size version for home printers and an answer key.

7. Christmas charades 5 min setup

Write things to act out on slips of paper: building a snowman, wrapping a present, a reindeer landing on the roof. Kids who can't read yet whisper with a grown-up partner. Teams of mixed ages work best.

8. Wrap the snowman 5 min setup

Teams get one roll of toilet paper and one volunteer. The first team to wrap their snowman from shoulders to shoes wins. Add a hat and a scarf at the end for a photo.

9. Christmas drawing game 5 min setup

One person draws a Christmas word on a big pad while their team guesses. Give little kids easy words (star, tree, present) and let a big kid draw for them if they want.

10. Who am I? 5 min setup

Stick a note with a Christmas character on each person's back: Rudolph, a gingerbread man, Frosty. They ask yes-or-no questions until they guess who they are. Works for ages six and up, or for little kids with a partner.

11. Gift exchange dice game 5 min setup

Everyone brings a small wrapped gift. Take turns rolling a die: a six means pass a gift left, a one means swap with anyone, and so on. Set a timer for ten minutes; whatever you're holding when it rings is yours.

12. Word search race for grandparents Printable

Hand out the same puzzle to grandparents and big kids and race to find every word. Large print makes it fair for older eyes. Our Large Print Christmas Word Search has big-letter puzzles with themes like Christmas Morning, Christmas Cookies and The Reindeer, one puzzle to a page.

Quick one-minute challenges

These fill the gap while someone checks the oven. Give each player one minute.

13. Cup tower No prep

Stack paper or plastic cups into a pyramid before the minute runs out. Little kids can just make the tallest tower they can.

14. Ornament spoon relay No prep

Carry an unbreakable ornament on a spoon across the room and back. Drop it and you start again from where you are, not from the beginning.

15. Cookie face No prep

Put a cookie on your forehead and get it to your mouth without using your hands. Older kids and adults love it, and little kids love watching.

A 45-minute party plan

  • As guests arrive: the giant word search on the wall, so early arrivals have something to do.
  • Minutes 0 to 15: freeze dance and the candy cane hunt for the little ones while grown-ups catch up.
  • Minutes 15 to 30: a whole-family game: charades or wrap the snowman in mixed-age teams.
  • Minutes 30 to 45: the gift exchange, then one-minute challenges until dinner.

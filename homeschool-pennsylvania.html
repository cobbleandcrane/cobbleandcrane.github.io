How to homeschool in Pennsylvania (2026-27)

Pennsylvania asks the most of any state on this list: a yearly affidavit or declaration, a portfolio, and an evaluation by a qualified evaluator every year. It is very doable once you know the calendar.

Key dates

  • Before you start (first year)File the affidavit or unsworn declaration with your school district's superintendent.
  • August 1, every later yearFile the affidavit or declaration again for the new school year (July 1 to June 30).
  • All yearKeep the portfolio: a reading log written as you go, plus samples of your child's work.
  • Grades 3, 5 and 8Add standardized test results (reading and math) to the portfolio.
  • June 30Submit the evaluator's written evaluation to the superintendent.

Who this applies to

Children from age 6 to 18. A child who is 6 on or before September 1 must start school or a home education program that year. Kindergarten is not required.

Step 1: File the affidavit or declaration

The parent running the program (the "supervisor") must have a high school diploma or its equivalent. File with the superintendent of your school district of residence. You can use a notarized affidavit or an unsworn declaration (no notary needed). The state publishes sample forms for elementary (K to 6) and secondary (7 to 12).

Attach:

  • An outline of proposed education objectives by subject
  • Proof of immunizations (or the exemption)
  • Proof of the required health and medical services (or the exemption)

District approval is not required to begin once the paperwork is filed.

What to teach

  • Elementary (K to 6): English (spelling, reading, writing), arithmetic, science, geography, U.S. and Pennsylvania history, civics, safety education including fire prevention, health and physical education, music, art
  • Secondary (7 to 12): English, mathematics, science, social studies (civics, world history, U.S. and Pennsylvania history), health, physical education, music, art

Either 180 days of instruction, or 900 hours (elementary) or 990 hours (secondary) a year.

What to keep: the portfolio

  • A log made at the time of instruction that names the reading materials by title
  • Samples of writings, worksheets, workbooks or creative materials
  • In grades 3, 5 and 8: results of the state tests (PSSA) or an approved nationally normed test in reading and math

If you count days, the log does not need hours per day.

End of year: the evaluation

A qualified evaluator interviews your child and reviews the portfolio, then certifies in writing whether an appropriate education occurred. Evaluators include licensed clinical or school psychologists, Pennsylvania-certified teachers, and nonpublic school teachers or administrators (see the state guide for the experience rules). You, or your spouse, cannot be the evaluator. Submit the evaluation to the superintendent by June 30.
